For this exercise it's important to remember, there's no such thing as a sustainable material. What makes something sustainable is the system built around that material/product. For example, paper and wood is only sustainable if we can regrow trees faster than we cut them down. So I'm not just looking at if materials are made of recyclable materials but if the product itself lets me reduce my overall waste and if I can reuse the product over and over again.

# Transport
I live in quite an average sized town in England. I could easily walk from my house to the other side of town if I wanted to. So I do not use my car if going somewhere within my town. However, there's not much in my town that interest me. So do use my car for places outside my town I visit regularly such as work, the Hackspace I go to, and bouldering.

I would like to use the train for these trips. But I find it's too expensive for regular trips. Taking the train once in a while seems fine. But even with Trump's war on Iran pushing up oil prices, taking a car is still cheaper.

Ideally, I'd like to live in a city with all the amenities I'd want close by. Great cycling infrastructure where I'd use a cargo-bike for any heavy loads I'd wanna carry. For trips outside the city, I'd either use a train or rent and electric car for places I can't access by train.
